# Vision

## Meaning

The Wolfsblvt Works website is the home of Wolf's software, experiments, and ideas: a place to encounter what he makes,
what drew him in, and what might be worth following next. It is a single long walk past projects, reasons and the
people working with him, ending at a pile of everything else and an open invitation to write. This Vision keeps the
whole destination of the site, and the feeling it should leave, so that each tranche of building, content and
redesign moves towards it rather than settling for a tidy studio brochure. It covers the website only; Wolfsblvt Works
itself, its principles and its voice are preserved in the Works foundation, and each project keeps its own Vision.

Status: Provisional

## Origin

The site grew out of the September 2026 co-design of the Wolfsblvt Works foundation. Wolf wanted Works to be a maker
identity with room for unlike things, and asked that it be described as "the home of Wolf's software, experiments, and
ideas: what he's making, what drew him in, and what might be worth following next." Many of his projects start with a
"what if…" or an "I need, but…", and the site was meant to carry that: some things exist because they are useful, some
because they were fun, some because a question would not leave him alone.

From that conversation came The Walk, the Pile and Yoru. The opening line, "Oddly useful ~~software.~~ things. also
games. also questions.", was Wolf's exact selection: confident, then visibly corrected outwards. The design was then
worked out over several rounds on a design canvas (`design/the-walk/`) before any production code existed.

The contact form arrived from the other direction. It was first built as a service in the `wolfsblvt.com` repository,
before this site was planned. Once the Works site took shape, Wolf decided that contact belongs to Works and moved the
service here, so every Wolfsblvt site links to one form, one inbox and one privacy text.

## What the site is for

Someone arrives curious: from a project, a profile link, a conversation, or nowhere in particular. They should leave
knowing what kind of maker this is, having found at least one thing they want to look at more closely, and knowing
that writing to Wolf about whatever is on their mind is genuinely welcome.

The site is not a portfolio grid, a company page or a sales funnel. It introduces work at Wolf's pace, in his voice,
and lets each project speak for itself once you are inside its window.

## The experience

- **A walk, not a menu.** One continuous page: the hero, the projects that deserve a proper introduction, why the work
  is made the way it is, who makes it, how to support it, the Pile, and hello. Separate pages exist only where
  something functional needs its own address.
- **A frame you walk along.** Two frayed lines in orange and magenta run beside the content and scroll with it. The
  background is a path through the work, not wallpaper.
- **Projects in their own voice.** Works narrates between them. Inside a project's window the project takes over, in
  its own type, colour and words. A precise technical tool and a playful game can sit side by side without being
  flattened into one card style.
- **Wolf's handwriting, used sparingly.** Rose handwriting is his: a correction, an aside, a joke. Rare enough that it
  always means a person is talking.
- **Yoru lives here.** The resident follows along, dozes, runs off when poked and naps on the footer. He makes the
  error pages and the legal pages feel like the same place.
- **The Pile.** An evolving collection of ideas, smaller projects, artifacts, notes and links, dealt fresh every day and
  arranged like things actually piled on a desk. Pieces vary in form on purpose: a sentence, a ticket, a card, a
  specimen, a door to a repository. Opening "There's more. A lot more." scatters it into Endless, the full collection,
  without leaving the walk. Public length is never a measure of how much thought sits behind a piece.
- **An open invitation.** Contact is for whatever occurs to the reader: an idea, a question, a connection, a hello. It
  never qualifies people as customers or collaborators first.
- **Support said plainly.** A real section explaining what sponsorship sustains, linking the routes that are actually
  enabled, neither hidden nor pleading.

## The whole destination

The current build is the shell, the mechanics and the first real content. The site grows towards:

- **Real projects, each properly introduced.** Every featured project with its own window, branding and voice, and its
  standing (released, in progress, paused, imagined) stated honestly. Featured projects change over time; the site's
  identity must not depend on whichever ones are current.
- **A full Pile.** Real pieces replacing the illustrative ones, growing continuously as an ordinary content addition,
  with pieces that can carry their own small interactions without each needing hand-built motion.
- **Yoru with more life.** Behaviour that responds to context, places he belongs, more animation. These are recorded
  possibilities from Wolf, not a specification.
- **One contact destination for every Wolfsblvt product.** Each product links to the Works contact page with its own
  `?product=` context; the page shows where the visitor came from and leads them back.
- **A fuller account of the people.** The site keeps a concise, demonstrated introduction of the collaborators; a
  fuller account belongs to a separate home such as the collective profile, not to extra pages here.

## Character that must survive

- Personal and definite. Opinions stated plainly, sincerity allowed to stand.
- Honest about what exists. A project's standing is said once; a composed scene is content, and only unreplaced
  scaffolding is labelled as such.
- Private by construction. No analytics, no tracking, no consent banner, nothing stored in the browser. Variety in the
  Pile comes from the date and what a request already carries, never from following a person.
- Dark only. The palette, the glow and the handwriting are designed on a dark canvas.
- Readable and usable without motion or JavaScript. Every animation has a finished state, and the content is there
  first.

## Not this

- A conventional multi-page studio site, a brochure, or a page per project.
- A public roadmap or backlog. The Pile is not a vote on what gets built next.
- A shop. Projects may mention commercial plans in context; buying happens on the project's own site.
- A showcase of AI. The collaborators are introduced because they are part of how the work is made, not as the subject.
