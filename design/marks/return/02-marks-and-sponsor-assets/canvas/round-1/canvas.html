Five ways Works could sign its name. and a banner that pops.

This is discovery, not a choice. Each direction is a different idea about what kind of signature Works is — carved, written, stamped, scribbled or lit — drawn far enough to judge at every size it has to live at: the formal lockup, a 16-pixel favicon, the 24-pixel wolfsblvt-icons slot, and a “by WOLFSBLVT” credit on someone else’s page. Below them are four sponsor treatments, tested on Works and on two very different product pages.

Press Replay on any board: the marks are drawn to move, because motion is part of the identity, not a garnish.

Held fixed (from the sources)

  • WOLFSBLVT dominant, WORKS subordinate. Kept in every direction.
  • No literal wolf, no Yoru face, no WW-by-default. Yoru appears only as a guest in one Works-native banner.
  • Smoke & Fireworks: orange from the left, magenta from the right, rose for handwriting, cyan for precise interaction.
  • Sponsor words are the selected ones, exactly. Two buttons. No prices, no logos, no counts.

Provisional (mine, to be joined later)

  • Lockups use live fonts as stand-ins: Literata, Albert Sans, Shantell Sans (the Walk’s current set). The chosen direction gets drawn letters in round 2.
  • Light-mode colours (paper #f6efe6, deeper ink relatives) are my authored guesses, not grammar decisions.
  • Host pages are sketches: diffdevil from its window in the Walk; the light page is illustrative, not smolpng’s real styling.

My recommendation

A · Chip-cut as the symbol, with D’s handwritten “works” kept as the expressive second voice (footer, sponsor art, the site header on large screens) — while the formal lockup stays typeset.

Why: the chip is the only symbol here that explains the palette instead of just wearing it. Orange and magenta stop being two brand colours and become two lights on one cut. It comes from the name’s own V, not a wolf or the Walk, so it survives a site redesign. At 16 px it degrades into a clean orange-magenta V, which is the best small-size behaviour of the five. It doesn’t lean on a W, a badge or a frame, which is where most of the other four meet familiar company.

Its weak spot is warmth; a carved triangle is precise and a little cool on its own. That’s exactly what the handwritten “works” supplies — the precise construction and the human correction, side by side, which is already the site’s best move (the crossed-out software).

Runner-up: C · Maker’s punch, if you want the credit on product pages to feel like a hallmark more than a logo. It is the best “by WOLFSBLVT” of the five.

What I need from you

  • Which symbol idea moves forward — or which two should fight one more round. Mixing is fine (e.g. A’s symbol with D’s lockup).
  • Handwriting: do you want the “works” scribble to be your handwriting? You said some site notes will be. If yes, write “works” ten-ish times on paper or a tablet and I vectorise the best one.
  • Sponsor on light pages: does Works bring its own night (the dark inset window) or re-light for daylight? Both are shown.
  • Which sponsor art — Lit edge, Fireworks, Carved field or Big type. They pair with different marks; Carved field only really sings with A.

Round 2 then builds the chosen family for real: drawn wordmark, optical 16/32 favicon cuts, the glyph through the icon-standard checks, the sponsor section as a reusable component plus the static README SVG, and the usage reference.
